# The Dutiful Child

A small figure standing east of [[The Mother]], built as she is and two thirds her height, with one arm raised and held out and the other fallen to his side. His feet are together. He has been standing that way for longer than anyone has been counting.

He is the [[The Ranjergon|Divine Gate]] — not a monument to it, not a symbol of it. The figure is the thing itself, as the sky shows it.

## The posture

Every other figure on the suns' road stands square or reaches with both arms. This one does not, and the asymmetry is the whole of the reading.

One hand is up and out: *stop*. One hand is down and open: *pass*. That is precisely and completely what the Gate does — divine influence reaches the mortal world through prayer, through clergy, through artifacts and the acts of the faithful, and the gods themselves do not follow it through. Outward in limited forms; inward not at all.

He is faint. Nothing in the figure is bright, and on a poor night he is not there at all. People have made something of this, and it is probably worth saying plainly that the Gate's dimness in the sky is not doctrine — but it has not stopped anyone observing that a barrier which has never once been seen by anybody is fittingly hard to find.

## Why he is called dutiful, and meant

The word is approving, and the people who use it most are the ones the Gate costs most. That requires explaining.

Ahvantir sits on a nexus. Traders, refugees, explorers, scholars and people pulled through tears in reality by accident have all come ashore here carrying their faiths with them, and **a single believer is enough**: any deity who holds one follower on Ahvantir gains an aspect and a place in [[The Ranjergon]]. The divine population of this world is not a pantheon that was assembled. It is an accumulation, and it has no ceiling.

Set that beside the obvious question. If every one of those gods walked this earth among the mortals who serve them, unchecked — the merciful beside the cruel, the orderly beside the ruinous, the peaceable beside gods whose entire portfolio is war — the result is not a crowded world. It is a battleground, and the mortals on it are the terrain. The last time the gods had free run of Ahvantir they fought a war across it so destructive that the conflict ground to a halt under its own weight.

Scholars of planar theology see this. So do most of the faithful. The separation is real and it is grieved — there are people in this city who will never in their lives receive a word they can be certain came from the god they serve — but the grief is not confusion about why the Gate exists. Ask a Pathite priest whether she would unmake it and the answer is no, and it is not a diplomatic no.

So the name holds in every tradition. The spirits watched it born and watched it hold. The [[Drorn'Duur]] inherited it with its mother and its story. Aru'Mas inherited it again, and calls it dutiful although it is their own gods it keeps out, because they have thought about the alternative.

## What he was made for, and what he does now

Worth noticing, and not often noticed: the Gate was born at the end of the [[The Ranjergon|Divine War]], before recorded history, to stop a war between the gods who were already here.

Nearly every deity it now holds back arrived afterwards, carried ashore in the faith of somebody who survived a crossing. [[Imwei, the Green Mother|Imwei]] and the unnamed spirit of compassion could not have been protecting Ahvantir from them; they had never heard of them. The Gate was made to end one war and has turned out to be the only thing standing between this world and a crowd that did not exist yet.

Whether the Gate understands that, nobody can say. It is sentient, it maintains the terms of its own existence with something approaching will, and the scholarly consensus on whether it can be reasoned with is that the attempt is either impossible or extremely dangerous. The rituals that reach the Gilded Pool do not breach it so much as petition it, and whether a petition succeeds appears to depend on the Gate's own disposition.

It has been breached once. The [[Orevicerium]] fragments that exist in the mortal world were torn from it during the Divinity Conflict, and they are the only pieces of him anyone has ever held.
