# The Crown

A band of three points standing in the far north, with the tallest of them carrying on up to the pole star. [[Marduk Sunspear|Marduk]] named it himself, and what he said about it has outlasted his house.

It is the duty of the royal family. It has never been the privilege, and it is one of only two figures in this sky that never set.

## The figure

Four stars and three lines: a band of three points, the middle one tallest, and a fourth line carrying on from that middle point to the pole.

The star at the end of that line is **Heaven's Light** — the pole star, a degree and a half off true north, magnitude −1.14 and tenth brightest in the sky. It is the star every pilot and every caravan master on the archipelago steers by, and its height above the horizon is your latitude. It had belonged to no figure at all until the Crown was drawn around it.

Everything in the band sits above declination fifty-four. From Aru'Mas that means none of it sets. The three points swing round the pole through the night, dropping to four degrees above the northern horizon at their lowest and climbing to seventy-six at their highest, and Heaven's Light sits almost still at forty degrees while they go round it.

**The suns do not pass through it.** They stand in its *sign*, the stretch of road named for it, from **30 Cindralis** to **11 Embrathen** — a stretch the Crown left behind. The figure is thirty degrees further north than the suns ever reach. It stands highest at midnight around **4 Ossandrel**, though for this figure that matters less than it does for the others, since it is never not there.

## The one that never goes off duty

Eleven of the thirteen rise and set. This is not one of them. Only the Coiled Serpent keeps it company up there, and nobody has yet attached a story to the Serpent, so the observation has always been made about the Crown.

That is a property of where the stars are and not of anything anyone decided, but it has not gone unremarked, and the remark is always the same. A crown that is never out of the sky is a crown nobody is ever off duty from. The figure is overhead at the hour the court sits and overhead at the hour it does not; it is there in the month of the royal observances and equally there in the months when nobody thinks about the palace at all.

Preachers have made the obvious use of it for four hundred years. So, less often and more quietly, have people with complaints about the palace.

## What Marduk said

> The heaviest burden fate could place upon a man, one which he must accept with strength and grace or not at all.

The phrasing is worth reading closely, because it is doing something careful.

It is not a boast. It does not say the crown is a reward, or that it is deserved, or that the man wearing it is equal to it. It says the thing is placed on you by fate, that it is heavy, and that there are exactly two ways to take it — properly, or not. There is no third option in the sentence where you take it badly and carry on.

For the founder of a dynasty, speaking about the office he had just invented for himself and intended to hand to his sons, that is a remarkable thing to have committed to. It is also, read coldly, a very good thing to have said: a king who announces that his crown is a weight has pre-emptively answered the people who were going to say he enjoys it.

Both readings survive. The city teaches the first.

## The house it was named for

The Sunspear line did not end well.

Marduk's throne passed to his elder son Darod and down the generations to Dammadan, by which time the dynasty and the city's story about itself had become hard to separate. Then the archangel [[Asgoraviel]] came, and the siege ran nine years, and Dammadan Sunspear and his entire court died in it. What held the city through the end of that was Dammadan's queen consort, **Nishta Ilderas-Sunspear**, who bore the Ilderas name before there was an Ilderas dynasty, and who handed a throne to a son whose first act was to rename the age.

So the man who said the crown must be accepted with strength and grace or not at all founded a house that was not given the choice. It was taken from them in a room, over nine years, by something that did not care how gracefully they carried anything.

The [[Ilderas Dynasty]] kept the sign. They did not rename it, and there is no record of anyone proposing they should.

## What it is used for now

Royal observances, coronations, oaths of office, and the swearing-in of anyone whose work involves standing between the Crown and somebody else. The figure is invoked at all of them, and the line about the burden is quoted at most.

It is quoted approvingly. That is the part that repays attention. A sentence written by a founder to describe his own weight has become a thing said *to* officials by the people appointing them, which is nearly the opposite use. Marduk was explaining himself. Four centuries later the same words are a reminder issued to somebody else, and the reminder has teeth his explanation never had.
