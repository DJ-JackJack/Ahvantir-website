# The Dreamer

A sleeper lying out along the suns' road, head east and feet west, with one arm fallen away beneath them. It is the sign of [[Nodd the Monarch of Dreams|Nodd]], and it is younger than most of the sky by a long way.

It is also a memorial, which not everyone who uses it for astrology remembers.

## The figure

Six stars and five lines. A body stretched east to west, and then the thing that makes it: an arm dropping thirty-nine degrees south and away. Without that fallen arm this is a line of stars with a kink in it. With it, somebody is asleep — not resting, not lying down on purpose, but gone slack in the way a sleeping arm goes over the edge of a bed.

Eleven stars sit in the sign and six are used.

The brightest of them is at the end of that fallen arm, magnitude −0.99 and fifteenth in the sky. The hand is the brightest part of the sleeper, which is a strange emphasis for a figure about sleep and a very apt one for a figure about reaching.

The suns walk through it from **19 Thirivale** to **3 Cindralis**, twenty-eight days. It stands highest at midnight around **6 Myrnselt**.

## Nodd

Nodd is the Warden-sovereign of [[The Dreaming|the Dreaming]], and they are not a deity. The distinction is not pedantry. A deity is sustained by worship and dies without it; Nodd is a spirit — a [[Loci]] of a scale nothing else in Ahvantir approaches, born of a place rather than a creature, and the place is the collective sleep of everyone who has ever slept here.

They were popular. That is the word for it. Humans liked them and spirits liked them, which is a short list of things true of both, and what made it possible was their power, their reach, and a neutrality that everyone was prepared to take at face value.

Whether that neutrality was real is a question nobody needed to ask until it was tested.

## The Shattered Strand

It was tested at the [[Shattered Strand]].

The short version, and the one the city tells: the early rulers of Aru'Mas wanted a peninsula that was not theirs to want. They sent workers and mages and surveyors to take it, in defiance of the pact that said they could not. The spirits answered in a single night. The sea came up, the land went under, and by morning there was a reef where a peninsula had been, with everyone who had been standing on it still there.

Nodd tried to intervene. That is the whole of what is recorded about their part in it — that they tried, and that it did not work.

## Why the figure was named

Afterwards, the sign was named for them.

It is a strange honour and it was meant to be. Nodd did not stop it. The intervention failed and the people on the Strand drowned, and the city that had caused the thing then put the name of the one who tried to prevent it into the heavens. The name honours Nodd, and the naming honours the fallen, and those are two different acts folded into one gesture.

Whether it also flatters the city is a question the city does not raise about itself.

## What it means to read

A figure that commemorates a failure sits oddly in a sky otherwise full of mothers, weapons and animals. Most astrology done with this sign treats it as it treats any other — sleep, dreaming, the passage of a thing from one state to another — and never touches the Strand at all.

That is not ignorance so much as drift. Four hundred years is long enough for a monument to become furniture. The sleeper goes on lying there with their arm fallen away under them, and the reef goes on being avoided by every ship with a pilot who knows the water, and the connection between the two is a thing you have to be told.
