# The Aloysius Don'Raffian Atripho Arcanomechanical Clock Tower

## The Tower

Eight sides, eight faces, one on each, so the hour can be read from any direction a person
approaches. The faces are backlit by radiant crystals that absorb sunlight across the day and give
it back as a steady glow, which means the tower is as legible at night as at noon. On a clear
evening it is the brightest fixed thing in the skyline.

It stands in Upper Hearthstone, on the elevated ground that gives the district its name and its
advantages, and from there it is visible across most of [[Aru'Mas]]. Most, not all. The city is
not evenly built, and there are quarters, courtyards and lanes where the Sentry simply cannot be
seen — a fact with more consequences than it first appears to have.

## Why the City Needed One

Aru'Mas has two suns. [[Solara and Nystara]] establishes that every object in open sunlight casts
two shadows, one warmer and softer from Solara's angle and one sharper and blue-edged from
Nystara's, and that where neither falls the light is at its brightest.

A sundial in this city therefore gives two answers, and they disagree. Shadow-reckoning works only
for someone who already knows which sun they are reading and where it stands, which is a
specialist's skill rather than a glance out of a window. Mechanical and arcane timekeeping in
Aru'Mas is not an ornament or a convenience. It is the only way an ordinary person gets a
straight answer about the hour.

## The Chime

The Sentry sounds, and the sound is not fixed. Its pitch, its tone and its melody all shift with
the season, the time of day and the weather, because the chime is generated by the amount and
quality of the sunlight the tower receives rather than struck from a fixed bell.

The practical effect is that people who grew up within earshot read the weather and the turn of the
year by ear without consciously attending to it. A thin, high chime means one thing about the day
ahead; a heavy and slow one means another. Visitors hear a pretty noise. Residents hear a report.

Because the light falling on the tower is compound rather than simple, the chime's character
follows which of the two suns is doing most of the work. It follows from the geometry that a
crossing — one sun passing behind the other, or a moon across either — must reach the chime as a
shift in voice. In Upper Hearthstone the sky is heard before it is looked at.

The chime carries through most of Upper Hearthstone. It does not carry across the city. The faces
serve Aru'Mas; the sound serves the district that holds the tower, which is a distinction the
district has never been shy about.

## The Second Purpose

The Sentry is a wizard's staff. Not in the sense of resembling one — in the sense of doing what one
does, at the scale of a building.

A staff gathers a caster's will and hands it back sharpened. The tower does that for any number of
House Atripho's wizards at once, taking their separate intents and binding them into a single
casting. Circle casting is ordinary enough among practitioners who can coordinate; circle casting
with no practical ceiling on the number of participants is a different proposition. Range and output
run to something near a hundred times what the same casters could manage unaided, and a spell with
an area can have that area spread across the whole of Aru'Mas.

Nor is the tower limited to what its casters bring. It draws on the city's magical reserves as well,
which leaves its effective supply all but bottomless. The constraint on a working performed from the
Sentry is not power. It is what its casters can agree to do.

That last part already has a name in the city's own records. [[The Arcanum Dispersal System]] runs
electrum conduits beneath the streets and through key structures, fed from the Arcanum Core and the
ley lines under Ahvantir, and it makes explicit provision for this: authorised users may tap a
conduit directly to recharge their reserves or amplify their spells, a practice the
[[Order of Wisdom]] monitors to prevent misuse. The Sentry is not a loophole in that arrangement.
It is that same provision, built into a tower.

## An Open Secret

None of this is concealed. Ask an artisan in Hearthstone about the Sentry and they may well tell you
it doubles as a great arcane focus, in the same tone they would use for the number of steps to the
gallery or the year the clock last stopped. It is a fact about the clock. It is interesting. It comes
up, and then the conversation moves on.

Nothing is hidden, and nothing is reckoned with either. A structure able to lay one spell over every
district in the city, powered past any meaningful limit, worked by a single noble house, stands in
open view above a district of stonemasons and weavers — and the city has agreed, without ever once
discussing it, that this is a piece of trivia. That is the most durable sort of secret there is. Not
the one nobody knows. The one everybody knows and nobody has thought about twice.

House Atripho's motto is *Knowledge Is Power, Power Is Masked*. The mask here is not an illusion, a
ward or a sealed chamber. It is a clock. The house built something enormous, told the city plainly
what it was, and let the city's own indifference do the concealing. The name says as much out loud:
*arcanomechanical*, two halves in that order, and Aru'Mas has only ever heard the second one.

The nickname fits better than the city means it to. A sentry keeps watch, which the tower does, with
eight faces and no blind side. A sentry is also posted, and armed, and waiting to be told.

## Aloysius Don'Raffian Atripho

Aloysius Don'Raffian Atripho was head of [[House Atripho]] until his assassination in 273 MC. The
tower was his personal project, and he did not live to see it finished.

Construction completed in 278 MC, five years after his death. The dedication is therefore
posthumous: the house carried the work to completion and named the finished tower in his honour,
dedicating it to his memory. Whatever he had intended to call it is not recorded, and the name it
carries is the one his house gave it once he could no longer object.

The project sits squarely within what House Atripho says it stands for. The house champions the
responsible use of enchantment in civil applications, and a public clock legible to the whole city
is that position made literal — arcane capability spent on something a labourer can use by
glancing up. The house also plays, by its own account, an influential role in citywide arcane
regulation — a comfortable position for the owners of the largest arcane instrument in Aru'Mas to
hold.

The house's standing rule that no heir may represent it until they have mastered a school of magic
to an advanced degree reads differently alongside a tower that combines any number of Atripho
casters into one working. Whether or not it was written for that, it supplies it.

## The Name Nobody Uses

Five names and a compound adjective, and the city reduced all of it to one word within a
generation. Everybody calls it the Sentry.

The nickname is not affectionate so much as accurate. The tower watches, it is tall, it does not
move, and it tells you where you stand in the day whether you asked or not. That the formal
dedication of a murdered man's life's work has been worn down to a single functional noun is the
sort of thing Aru'Mas does without noticing, and the sort of thing his house has never publicly
remarked on.

## Timekeeping in Aru'Mas

The city reckons a twenty-four hour day. The Sentry is the central mechanism by which that
reckoning is made public, though not the only one; Aru'Mas keeps time by several devices, and the
Sentry is the one everybody can see.

Which makes it civic infrastructure rather than a monument. Two people who have never met can
agree on a moment by naming an hour, anywhere the faces can be read — markets open against it,
shifts change against it, and the criminal vocabulary of [[Black Marks]] uses it to turn a tally
beside a mark into an appointment. Where the Sentry cannot be seen, all of that falls back on
older and worse arrangements.
