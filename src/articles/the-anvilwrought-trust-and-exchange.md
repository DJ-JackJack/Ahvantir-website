# The Anvilwrought Trust and Exchange

(Colloquially: “The Anvil”)

## Overview

The Anvilwrought Trust and Exchange, commonly called The Anvil, is the official banking institution of Aru’Mas. Owned and operated by [[House Athram]], it is the foundation of the city’s financial system and a symbol of dwarven stability. From safeguarding noble heirlooms to financing mercantile ventures, The Anvil underpins much of Aru’Mas’ prosperity.

Its influence reaches into every district, and the weight of its vault keys is rivaled only by the crown itself. The Anvil deals directly only with nobles, royalty, trade partners from other worlds, and the cream of the crop among adventurers, many of them with more money than sense. Everyone else banks through the [[Merchants Guild|Merchants’ Guild]] and the [[Adventurers Guild Association]], whose banking services rely on The Anvil’s backing and vault structure while sparing it direct dealings with clientele it deems beneath its notice. This arrangement reinforces the institution’s aura of exclusivity and prestige.

## Structure

Grand Hall: The headquarters of The Anvil resides in the [[Crowns Approach]]. The structure resembles a fortress more than a counting house, with walls of black granite and doors reinforced with dwarven runes. Beneath its facade lies the largest secured gateway into the Vault Works.

District Branches: Every official district of Aru’Mas hosts a branch, scaled to its wealth. Branches are closed to the public; they serve as clearinghouses for the guild banking desks in their district, settling accounts, moving coin, and providing access to the vaults. In affluent districts like the [[Luminous Reach]], branches resemble temples of commerce; in [[The Spillway|the Spillway]], they are little more than austere offices where coin is weighed and recorded.

Leadership: Operation of The Anvil is entrusted to the Silthro family, a lesser family held under the [[House Athram]] name. The Anvil is run by Garrod Balisong Varivance Silthro-Athram, Grand Chancellor of Holdings.

Anvil Clerks: Employees of The Anvil are marked by House Athram’s crest, sworn to secrecy through magically binding contracts. They are as much bureaucrats as priests, reciting oaths of integrity before every day’s work.

## Services

Depository Accounts: Direct clients deposit coin and valuables with The Anvil and receive Crownnotes, redeemable at any branch. Ordinary citizens deposit through guild banking desks into communal reserves held under Anvil backing, and receive stamped Ledger Slips issued by the guild.

Private Vaults: Nobles, guilds, and organizations may secure private vaults. High-ranking members of the Adventurers Guild Association may apply for vault access, though The Anvil reviews each applicant’s wealth before granting it. A vault elevates an adventurer’s social status. Ordinary citizens may not hold private vaults.

Loans and Credit: The Anvil provides structured loans, secured by collateral or magical bonds. Adventurers often pledge future contracts, while guilds pledge shares of trade.

Currency Exchange: Works in concert with the Merchants’ Guild to evaluate foreign coinage, charging a standard appraisal fee. Everyday appraisal takes place at Merchants’ Guildhalls (see [[Currency of Aru'Mas]]); The Anvil handles exchange directly only for its own clients, such as trade partners from other worlds.

Letters of Exchange: Recognized city-wide and among foreign trade partners, these notes allow merchants to transfer wealth without transporting coinage.

Minting: The Anvil strikes all coinage of Aru’Mas under royal charter (see [[Currency of Aru'Mas]]).

Writs of Debt: The Anvil ultimately pays out every Writ of Debt, though the receiver may present one at any desk they have access to, whether a guild banking desk or The Anvil itself (see [[Currency of Aru'Mas]]).

Lockboxes and Safekeeping: Private chambers and lockboxes, often warded with runic and spiritual protections, for artifacts, heirlooms, and sensitive contracts.

## Security

Runic Locks: Vaults are sealed with Athram-crafted wards, requiring layered authentications.

Stoneborn Guardians: Dwarven sentinels, warforged protectors, and even animated statues serve as guardians of the halls.

The Eternal Ledger: A magical tome networked across branches records transactions in real time, exposing fraud and forgery instantly.

Spirit Wards: House Athram has bound [[Hearth Spirits|hearth spirits]] and omens to safeguard prosperity. Clients often describe the uncanny feeling of being “watched” within the halls.

## The Vault Works

The vaults of The Anvil do not exist beneath Aru’Mas but within an extradimensional space known as the Anvilwrought Excavation and Vault Works, commonly called the Vault Works. Workers and those who have experienced it firsthand often call it simply “The Labyrinth.” Discovered and stabilized by House Athram, this mysterious plane consists of endless halls and chambers into which new vaults are carved.

The specifics of its place within Ahvantir’s cosmology remain unknown, though House Athram claims to have mapped several miles of secure passages. Rumors suggest that some of the wealth of The Anvil was never deposited at all, but instead taken from vaults already present within the Vault Works. Whispers among the wary speculate on what may happen should the original owners of those vaults return to reclaim their treasures.

## Influence

The Anvil is as much a political force as a financial one. By controlling access to vaults, loans, and letters of exchange, House Athram holds quiet leverage over other noble houses, guilds, and even the crown.

Nobility: Many noble houses entrust family artifacts and heirlooms to Anvil vaults, ensuring House Athram has influence over even the oldest bloodlines.

The Church: [[The Church of the Threefold Path]] tolerates The Anvil, provided its practices avoid exploitative usury. House Athram carefully balances profit with restraint.

Commonfolk: Ordinary citizens never deal with The Anvil directly, but its backing stands behind the guild desks where they bank. Depositing even a few Lions is considered a step toward legitimacy, while holding a private vault key is a sign one has “made it” in Aru’Mas.
