# The Mother

A standing woman, taller than anything else on the suns' road, with her arms spread and falling open and her feet planted apart. She reaches from low in the south almost to the top of the sky, and the brightest star in that quarter of the heavens is her trailing foot.

She is [[Imwei, the Green Mother|Imwei]], and she has been called nothing else by anyone.

## The figure

The shape is unmistakable once it has been pointed out and difficult to find before that, which is true of most of the sky and especially true of her. There is a spine of three stars running almost straight down, shoulders across it, and two long arms that fall away to either side rather than reaching out — the posture of someone offering rather than someone taking. The legs are uneven. The western one is longer, and the foot at the end of it is the fiercest star for a wide stretch of sky.

The suns pass through her once a year, entering her stars in the last days of Varenthal and leaving them in the fourth turn of Ossandrel.

## What happens on her holy day

[[Imwei, the Green Mother|Imwei]]'s observances fall on the four pivots of the year — the two solstices and the two equinoxes — and one of those pivots falls inside her own figure.

On the equinox when the suns turn north, **12 Ossandrel**, the suns stand among her stars. The figure is not there. It is somewhere behind the daylight, washed out, and nothing a druid circle does that day will make it visible.

They gather anyway, and they do not treat it as a loss. The reasoning is old and is repeated to anyone who asks: Imwei cannot be seen in the mortal world at all, by the terms she herself made. A worshipper who needs to look at her has misunderstood what she is. That the one day of the year her figure vanishes is also one of her four holy days is regarded as a joke she is in on.

Six months later, on the equinox when the suns turn south, she is directly opposite them and stands at her highest at midnight. That is **33 Lochenvir**. Those who keep both days observe that the Mother is furthest from sight on the morning of the year's waking, and nearest to overhead on the night of its turning down.

## Why the name never changed

Almost every figure in the sky carries two or three names, one from each tradition that has looked at it. The Mother carries one.

The spirits call her the Mother. The [[Drorn'Duur]] called her the Mother. Aru'Mas calls her the Mother. The word differs between languages; nothing else does.

This is not an accident of translation. The spirits were present at the [[The Ranjergon|Divine War]] she ended — they were the other side of it — and they have never had cause to revise what they call her. The Drorn'Duur took the story whole from the spirits they were in pact with. The settlers took it again. All three describe the same event in the same terms, and there is no version of it in which she is anyone else.

Figures get renamed when somebody stands to gain by it. Nobody has ever stood to gain by renaming the Green Mother.

## Worship at the figure

Her followers have no temples and no icon, and the absence of an icon is doctrine rather than oversight: to fix Imwei in a single image is to misunderstand her. Some outside her faith have asked how a constellation escapes that objection, given that it is a fixed shape with a name.

The answer her circles give is that a constellation is not an image anyone made. It is a likeness found in the world, which is precisely the practice — reading the turn of the seasons, the behaviour of animals, the length of a shadow — that her worship consists of. A figure recognised in the stars is the same act as a hart stepping out of the treeline at a moment that feels deliberate.

Offerings made under her figure follow the ordinary rule: planted, buried, or put into moving water. Nothing is burned. What is given is meant to return to the world rather than leave it.
