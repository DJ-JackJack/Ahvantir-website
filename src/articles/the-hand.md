# The Hand

An open hand reaching upward with the fingers spread, wrist low in the south and the fingertips almost directly overhead. The star at its knuckles is the fourth brightest in the sky, and once it has been pointed out it is very difficult to see this part of the heavens as anything else.

It is named for the Hand of Gahr, which is said to have been pinned there.

## The figure

Eight stars and seven lines. A short forearm runs up from the wrist through a lower joint to the knuckles, and from the knuckles two long fingers rise and spread — each of two segments, the western one reaching highest. A thumb is thrown off the lower joint and away to the west, which is what settles the gesture: without it the shape reads as a fork or a branch, and with it the hand is unmistakably a hand, and unmistakably open.

Sixteen stars were available in this stretch of the suns' road and eight are used. The rest are left out on purpose. A hand drawn with every star inside its reach becomes a mitten.

The knuckle star carries magnitude −1.32 and is the brightest thing for a long way in any direction. The fingertips are fainter but not faint, and the whole figure climbs high: from Aru'Mas the western fingertip passes within two degrees of the zenith, so that for a few minutes each night in the right season a watcher has to lean back and look straight up to find the top of it. The wrist never climbs past about fifty degrees. The figure stands up out of the southern sky rather than lying along it.

The suns walk through it from **6 Myrnselt** to **34 Myrnselt**, twenty-nine days. It stands highest at midnight around **3 Cindralis**, half a year away, which is when anyone who wants to look at it properly should.

## The Hand of Gahr

The relic was a hand, or took the shape of one: a thing of spirit make, summoned rather than carried, which could be called up at a distance from whoever called it and made to close. What is agreed about it is that it could crush an army. What is agreed about how is nothing.

Scholars who have looked into it compare it to the conjured hands that any sufficiently practised wizard can still raise — the same trick of a grip that is not attached to an arm, made very much larger and very much stronger. The comparison is a guess dressed as a conclusion. The working of the thing did not survive, and neither did any account detailed enough to reconstruct it.

Gahr is not explained anywhere that survives. The name sits on the relic and goes no further.

## The Shale Bones

The last time anyone claimed to see it was at the Battle of the Shale Bones, early in the [[History of Ahvantir#The Landing War (-13 to 0 MC)|Landing War]].

The story told in Aru'Mas is short and it is told the same way every time. The hand was summoned. It came up out of the ground, or down out of the air, depending on who is telling it. [[Marduk Sunspear|Marduk]] drew back his spear and cast it upward, and the hand was pinned open against the sky, and it has hung there since with its fingers spread and unable to close.

That is a good story. It explains why the figure is an *open* hand — a closed one would have been a fist, and a fist is what the relic was for. It explains why the brightest star in the figure sits at the knuckles, where a spear through the back of a hand would go. It explains the sky in terms of the war, which was the point of telling it.

## What probably happened

The relic was destroyed in the fighting, and the sky had nothing to do with it.

This is the reading most people land on once they have thought about it for a minute, and it is not a modern scepticism. The battle was a bad one. Things were lost in it. A weapon that could crush an army is exactly the kind of thing that does not survive the day it is used, and a city with a founder to celebrate is exactly the kind of city that would rather say it was nailed to heaven than say it broke.

Both accounts agree on the only verifiable part, which is that nobody has seen the Hand of Gahr since.

## An old figure with a young name

Taken literally, the story dates the figure to the opening years of the Landing War — a little over four hundred years ago, and well inside the span the city can count.

The stars are considerably older than that, and so is the habit of looking at them. The spirits were reading this sky before the settlers arrived, and the [[Drorn'Duur]] were reading it after them and before the city. Whatever those two traditions called this group of eight stars, Aru'Mas does not have it and neither do we.

That absence is not surprising and it is not accidental. Much of what the Drorn'Duur knew was buried on purpose by a city that preferred its founding told as a landing and a pact, and a name for a constellation is a small thing to lose beside everything else that went with it. Some of what they knew was kept anyway, by people who went looking and paid for asking. None of what was kept includes this.

So the figure is old and the name is young, and the name is a claim. That is worth knowing before taking the shape as evidence for the story. The hand was in the sky first.
