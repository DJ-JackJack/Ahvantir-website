# Ahvantirian Fairy

## Born of Wanderlust

Ahvantirian fairies are fey born from the wanderlust of adventurers. Wherever explorers carry their hunger for the unknown into forgotten and dangerous places, that restless excitement lingers after them, and given enough time it gathers into a living thing. Most fairies awaken in ruins, flooded vaults, collapsed temples, and the deep places of the world, in the quiet that follows the passage of someone who should not have been there.

## No Two Alike

No two fairies look alike. Some resemble polished beetles or bright-feathered birds; others are leggy, masked, or strangely jointed, as though assembled from half-remembered stories. The only traits they share are their tiny size and a set of insect wings, whether gossamer, scaled, or hard-shelled. A fairy can grow to the height of a small child when it needs to stand its ground, but it is most at home darting through the air, small enough to hide in a coat pocket.

## What They Carry

The places that birth fairies are seldom kind, and not every adventurer leaves them alive. A fairy that awakens as an adventurer dies nearby sometimes takes something of that person into itself: a habit of speech, a fondness for a particular song, the face of a stranger, or a sudden certainty about where the treasure was buried. A few carry far more, and wake grieving for friends they have never known or answering to a name that was never theirs. Scholars of the fey disagree on whether these fairies carry the dead or merely their echoes. The fairies themselves rarely care to settle the question.

## Bound to Nothing

Most fey are bound to their courts, their groves, or their bargains. Ahvantirian fairies are bound to nothing. They wander as naturally as they breathe and are drawn to adventurers the way moths are drawn to light, often attaching themselves to a passing band of explorers with a quick eye, a sharp tongue, or simply their company. They do not always ask permission. Many adventuring companies can name the night a fairy "joined" them and the morning they realized it was not leaving.

A court is not a place so much as an allegiance. The fey of the [[Vast Green]]
organise into them — minor courts answering to lesser, lesser to greater, and all
of them at last to the Summer, Winter, Autumnal and Spring Courts that rule the
Green. Each is composed of its Lords and Ladies of Emotion, nobles who embody a
primal feeling and can embody nothing else. Allegiance shifts, fealty is traded,
and the whole structure sits beneath the King of Spirits, whom every spirit is
bound to obey.

Ahvantirian fairies stand outside all of it. Not in defiance — defiance would
require caring about the thing defied — but because the structure has never had
hold of them.

## The Green They Never See

Fey are spirits of feeling. They form where emotion gathers thickly enough to
take on a life, and the Vast Green, a plane steeped in raw emotion, is where
they belong. That is as true of Ahvantirian fairies as of any fey: the Green is
their true home.

They are not born there. An Ahvantirian fairy wakes on the Material Plane,
always — in a ruin, a flooded vault, a collapsed temple, wherever a mortal's
hunger for the unknown has soaked into a place and set.

And few of them ever see the home that is theirs. The next adventure is always
calling, drawing them on toward somewhere they have not been yet. It is not a
choice they are making and declining to revisit. It is what they are. A fairy
that settled in the Green would have to stop wanting the next unexplored thing,
and wanting it is the whole of what gathered it into being.

## Minor, and Unhurried

Fairies are minor spirits. They are spirits nonetheless, and the word in
Ahvantir does not sort things by power — Nodd is a spirit, and outmatches most
of the deities currently worshipped.

They do not age. Left alone, a fairy simply continues; what ends one is always
something that ended it, never time. Since nothing holds them anywhere for long,
a fairy's history is a list of places rather than years.

## In Aru'Mas

They reach the city the way they reach anywhere: attached to someone who was
going there. A company comes back from the Fend or somewhere further out, and
something comes back with them.
