# Lamplighters Guild

The Lamplighters Guild is not a military force. It is closer to a police force, and closer still to
something the city has never quite found a word for: a body of people who walk the roads of the
[[Fend and the Fendfolk|Fend]] and keep what lives out there from settling where the carts pass.

---

## The Sigil Posts

The guild's founding and still-primary mandate is the maintenance of the **Sigil Posts**, known to almost
everyone who relies on them simply as the **Lamps**.

They are not lamps. A Sigil Post is a piece of built magical technology whose purpose is to hold
hostile spirits at bay, and it lights nothing. The name is colloquial and comes from the soft blue
glow a working Post gives off, which is incidental to its function and happens to be the only way a
passing traveller can tell one is working at all.

The Posts line the roadways throughout the Fend. They do not wall anything off and they do not banish
anything. What they do is make a stretch of road an unattractive place for dangerous spirits to gather, and
gathering is the problem. A single hostile spirit on an open road is a hazard. Several of them collecting at
the same bend, night after night, becomes a reason the road stops being used, and a Fendtown cut off from
the road is a Fendtown that stops paying its levy and stops sending its grain.

The Posts need tending. Sigils wear, weather, and are defaced. A lapsed Post is not immediately obvious to
a traveller and is extremely obvious to whatever had been keeping its distance from it. Most of the guild's
working hours, in any given month, still go to the unglamorous business of walking a line of Lamps and
making sure each one is doing what it is supposed to do.

The Lamps have nothing to do with the lighting of [[Aru'Mas]] itself, which the
[[The Arcanum Dispersal System|Arcanum Dispersal System]] handles and which requires no Lamplighter
involvement. The two are separate technologies serving unrelated purposes, and the confusion between
them is a persistent annoyance to the guild.

---

## The Mandate, and How It Grew

Lamp maintenance was the whole of the original charge. It did not stay that way.

The guild's members were, by the nature of the work, the people in the Fend who understood spirits best and
were nearest to hand when something went wrong. Fendfolk began calling on them for matters no one had
assigned them. Over generations that became expectation, and expectation became duty. The Lamplighters now
answer for three things:

- **The Posts.** The original mandate, still the bulk of the work.
- **Disputes between Fendfolk and spirits.** A pact gone sour, a boundary crossed, an offering stopped, an
  obligation that one party reads differently from the other. The guild investigates, and where it can,
  settles. Much of this is closer to arbitration than to enforcement.
- **Spirits that have become too dangerous to coexist with.** The end of the escalation, and the part of
  the job that kills Lamplighters.

Nothing in the guild's charter was ever amended to cover the second and third of these. They are simply
what the guild does now, and the fact that no one ever wrote them down is part of why the funding argument
has never been resolved.

## Composition

The guild recruits for capability against spirits rather than for martial skill in general. Its ranks are
drawn primarily from **rangers, druids, clerics and paladins**, the traditions that produce people able to
track, treat with, ward against and, at need, destroy a hostile spirit.

Alongside them sits a much smaller body of **wizards and artificers**, who do not generally take the roads.
Their work is to furnish the guild with the equipment needed to fight violent spiritual forces at all, which
is specialised, expensive, and not available on the open market. The guild's dependence on a handful of
such people is one of its sharper structural weaknesses.

---

## Squads

Lamplighters are organised into **squads**. A squad runs between **four and twelve members**.

Squad size on paper and squad size in the field are very different numbers. A job will rarely see more than
**five or six** Lamplighters on it, and even that is uncommon, reserved for work the guild expects to be
exceptionally dangerous. The ordinary deployment is **two**. Spirit trouble in the Fend does not arrive on a
schedule or wait to be processed, so a pair who happen to be nearest are dispatched to whatever has come up
in their vicinity, and they handle it or they send for more.

Each squad is nominally responsible for an area of the Fend. In practice those jurisdictional lines are
ill-defined and largely ignored. Nobody in the guild treats them as a reason not to answer a call, and
nobody outside the guild has ever been able to say with confidence whose stretch of road a given Post sits
on.

---

## Command

Each squad has a **Squad Commander**, who leads it in the field and serves as its liaison to the guild's
leadership. The chain is short: the Commander is the only member of a squad who routinely deals with
anyone above it.

That leadership is known as **the Board**, and it operates out of **Lamplight Tower**, a complex in the
Fend that serves as the guild's headquarters. Squad Commanders return to the Tower to make their reports,
requisition supplies, and request assistance when a job has outgrown the people on it.

The Board is **extremely secretive**. Its members' names and faces are concealed by powerful illusory
magic, and not only from the guild beneath them: the Board's members are obscured **from each other**.
Whatever reason there is for that arrangement has never been made public, and the guild's rank and file
have no more idea who is giving them their orders than anyone outside the guild does.

The Board is also understood to be in **communication with the [[Fadu-Asai]]**, the spirit-side enforcers
who judge and punish spirits that break their pacts. What passes between them, how often, and how
cooperative any of it is are all unknown. A mortal guild that polices spirits in the Fend holding an open
channel to the spirit world's own arbiters of pact law is either the most sensible arrangement in the
archipelago or something considerably stranger, and nothing in the record settles which.

---

## Funding

The guild is short of money and short of people, and has been for as long as anyone can remember.

The cause is a jurisdictional argument that has outlasted several generations of the people having it.
[[Aru'Mas]] furnishes the Fendtowns with roadways, their upkeep, and Lamplighter patrols, in exchange for
good trade relations and a nominal levy. The city's position is that the levy is nominal precisely because
the Fendtowns benefit most directly, and that communities protected by the Lamps should carry a larger
share of what the Lamps cost. The Fendtowns' position is that the roads exist to move the city's food into
the city, that the service is one the city contracted to provide, and that raising the levy would be the
city charging them for a promise it already made.

Neither side is obviously wrong. Both sides have been saying it for centuries. The practical result is a
guild that does dangerous, expanding, largely undocumented work with a budget sized for lamp maintenance.
