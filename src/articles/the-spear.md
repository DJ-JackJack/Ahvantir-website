# The Spear

A leaf-shaped blade of four stars with a long straight haft running out behind it, lying along the path the suns walk. It is one of the few figures in Ahvantir's sky that nearly everyone can find, and the only one the suns themselves pass through.

It has been a weapon for as long as anyone has been looking at it. What has changed is whose.

## The Lancer

The oldest name in use is not a dead one. The spirits call the figure **the Lancer**, and they have not stopped — a Warden asked today will give that name without hesitation and without any particular interest in the city's opinion. Spirits do not revise themselves. Whatever the Lancer meant when the name was first used, it means now.

This makes Ahvantir's sky unusual. In most places the older names for the stars have to be dug out of ruins and guessed at. Here the first tradition is the one most easily checked: you can go and ask it. The newest tradition, correspondingly, is the one most easily caught out.

## The Warrior

The [[Drorn'Duur]] inherited the figure, as they inherited most of their sky, from the spirits they had spent centuries in pact with. They called it **the Warrior**. The shift is slight — a weapon becomes the one who carries it — and that slightness is the point. Stone-priests who worshipped with open flame under the stars were not inventing a sky; they were learning one that was already in use and putting it in their own mouths.

Most of the figures survive that way. The shape is the sky's and does not move; the name takes a step each time it changes hands, and rarely more than a step.

## The Spear

The settlers inherited it a second time, and in 0 MC they claimed it.

The claim was not a new constellation. That is what makes it work. Aru'Mas's astrologers did not draw a figure and announce it — they pointed at one that had been a warrior with a long weapon for as long as the Drorn'Duur had been naming it, and said that it was Marduk. Marduk Sunspear, whose own name is a spear, whose legion carries spears warded to burn with Solara's light. It is barely a lie. Barely a lie is what persuades.

What gave them the opening was the sky itself. In the year the [[First Pact]] was signed and the [[The Primordem|Primordem]] were sealed beneath the city, Solara passed entirely in front of Nystara — the two suns merged into a single disc, and stayed merged for five and a half days. It happened in that part of the sky. Whether it fell on the night of the sealing or merely near enough for the distance to be forgotten, it was close enough to be told as one event, and it was told that way within a generation.

The astrologers who told it that way did well out of the telling. They were courtiers of a government four hundred days old, in a city that had just finished a war it preferred not to describe accurately, and they offered it a heaven that agreed. Marduk's spear, burned into the sky above the place where the thing beneath the city had been put to sleep, watching over his people from above.

## What the suns do

The figure lies along the ecliptic, so the suns enter it once a year and leave it again. They stand deepest within it on **34 Lochenvir**, the same date every year, which is the date of the merging in 0 MC.

The anniversary therefore keeps itself. Nobody had to establish an observance or maintain a calendar for it; the suns return to the place on their own and the city has four centuries of habit built on top of that.
